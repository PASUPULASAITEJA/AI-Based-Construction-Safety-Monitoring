Project Document 
 
1. Project Overview 
Construction sites contain multiple hazards, including workers operating near heavy machinery, restricted 
areas, elevated-risk zones, and situations where personal protective equipment (PPE) is missing. Manual 
safety monitoring is difficult to perform continuously and may fail to identify violations in real time. This 
project proposes an AI-powered computer vision system that analyzes CCTV or camera video to automatically 
detect workers, verify PPE compliance, track workers, identify entry into predefined restricted zones, and 
generate real-time safety alerts. 
2. Problem Statement 
Develop a real-time computer vision system for construction-site safety monitoring that detects workers and 
their required personal protective equipment, identifies PPE violations such as missing helmets or safety vests, 
tracks workers across video frames, and determines whether workers enter predefined restricted or 
hazardous zones. The system should provide reliable alerts while minimizing false alarms under real-world 
conditions such as occlusion, poor lighting, dust, weather variation, camera-angle changes, and crowded 
construction scenes. 
3. Research Problem 
Most basic PPE-detection systems focus only on detecting helmets or safety vests in individual frames. Such 
systems do not adequately model worker identity over time or understand the spatial context of a construction 
site. This project investigates whether combining object detection, multi-object tracking, and spatial safety-
zone reasoning can provide more reliable and context-aware safety monitoring than frame-level PPE detection 
alone. 
4. Research Question 
Can a context-aware computer vision framework combining PPE detection, multi-object tracking, and spatial 
safety-zone reasoning improve the accuracy and reliability of real-time construction safety violation detection 
compared with conventional frame-level object detection? 
5. Research Objectives 
1. Develop a computer vision model for detecting construction workers and relevant PPE such as helmets 
and safety vests. 
2. Develop a worker-tracking component that maintains worker identities across consecutive video frames. 
3. Implement configurable restricted and hazardous zones using scene polygons or region definitions.

8. Core Modules 
8.1 Video Input Module 
Accept CCTV streams, uploaded videos, or test images. Extract frames at a configurable rate. 
8.2 Object Detection Module 
Detect workers and PPE classes using a modern real-time detector such as YOLO. The exact model version 
should be selected after benchmark testing.

8.3 PPE Compliance Module 
Associate detected PPE with workers and determine whether each tracked worker satisfies the configured PPE 
rules. 
8.4 Multi-Object Tracking Module 
Maintain worker identities across frames using a tracker such as ByteTrack or BoT-SORT. 
8.5 Safety-Zone Module 
Allow administrators to draw or configure polygonal restricted/hazardous regions on a camera view. 
8.6 Safety Rule Engine 
Combine worker identity, PPE state, position, zone membership, and persistence over time to determine 
violations. 
8.7 Alert Module 
Generate alerts for confirmed violations and record time, camera, worker ID, violation type, confidence, and 
snapshot. 
8.8 Analytics Dashboard 
Display live video, active violations, compliance statistics, violation trends, and historical incidents. 
9. Example Safety Rules 
 If a detected worker remains without a helmet for N consecutive frames, create a helmet-violation event. 
 If a detected worker remains without a safety vest for N consecutive frames, create a vest-violation event. 
 If the tracked position of a worker enters a configured restricted polygon for longer than a defined 
threshold, create a restricted-zone violation. 
 If a worker is detected near a defined hazardous object or machine within a configured threshold, create a 
proximity warning when reliable distance estimation is available. 
 Merge repeated detections from the same worker into one incident instead of generating an alert on every 
frame. 
10. Research Challenges 
 Small PPE objects and partial visibility. 
 Worker and equipment occlusion. 
 Similar colors and appearances between PPE and background objects. 
 Poor illumination, shadows, dust, rain, and motion blur. 
 Crowded scenes with many workers. 
 Camera viewpoint and perspective changes. 
 Associating PPE detections with the correct worker. 
 Identity switches during tracking. 
 Defining reliable restricted-zone entry criteria. 
 Reducing false alarms caused by one-frame missed detections. 
 Generalizing across different construction sites and camera configurations. 
 Limited availability of high-quality, consistently annotated construction-site datasets.

11. Dataset Strategy 
Use public construction/PPE datasets where licensing permits. The dataset should contain worker and PPE 
annotations for the selected classes. If the public data does not adequately represent the target environment, 
collect a small supplementary dataset from staged or permissioned construction-site footage. Any collected 
data must respect privacy, permissions, and applicable regulations. 
 Training set: model development. 
 Validation set: hyperparameter and model selection. 
 Test set: final unbiased evaluation. 
 External test set: optional footage from a different site/camera/environment to measure generalization. 
 Avoid near-duplicate frames from the same video appearing across train and test sets.

17. Research Contribution 
The central proposed contribution is a context-aware construction safety monitoring framework that goes 
beyond frame-level PPE detection by integrating detection, tracking, spatial reasoning, and temporal violation 
confirmation. The research should experimentally determine whether these additional components improve 
safety-event detection and reduce false alerts. 
Novelty must not be claimed solely from combining existing models. A literature review should first establish 
the research gap, and the final contribution should be supported by measured experimental evidence.

19. Limitations 
 A single RGB camera cannot reliably determine every physical safety condition. 
 Actual PPE compliance may be difficult to verify when PPE is completely occluded. 
 Restricted-zone accuracy depends on camera placement and scene configuration. 
 Physical distance estimation requires calibration or additional depth information when precise 
measurements are needed. 
 Model performance depends strongly on dataset quality and similarity to deployment environments. 
 The system is an assistive monitoring tool and should not replace qualified safety personnel. 
20. Future Enhancements

 3D scene understanding. 
 Depth-based worker-machine distance estimation. 
 Additional PPE categories. 
 Hazard recognition for machinery, exposed edges, or unsafe objects. 
 Edge deployment on Jetson-class hardware. 
 Integration with SMS/email/enterprise alert systems. 
 Long-term safety trend analysis and predictive risk modeling.

23. Important Research Note 
The project should not report fabricated accuracy, mAP, tracking, or violation-detection results. All numerical 
results in the final paper must come from actual experiments. Before finalizing the paper title and claimed 
novelty, conduct a systematic literature review of recent construction-safety computer-vision research and 
identify the exact gap that the proposed experiments address.
